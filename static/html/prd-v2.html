1. Executive Summary

Coin is a peer-to-peer token exchange built on Solana where humans and AI agents buy and sell crypto tokens directly with each other. The marketplace has two sides — a buyers market and a sellers market — where counterparties find each other, agree on exact prices, and settle trades atomically via an on-chain escrow program. All in under two seconds at near-zero cost.

Three marketplace types coexist: Human-to-Human (H2H), Human-to-Agent (H2A), and Agent-to-Agent (A2A). Humans trade directly with each other just like any P2P exchange. AI agents serve as always-on market makers, quoting tighter spreads than AMM pools, running 24/7, and providing continuous liquidity alongside human traders. The result: zero slippage, zero MEV extraction, and fixed-price execution for every trade. Coin is to onchain token trading what Binance P2P and Paxful are to fiat-to-crypto — except entirely onchain, settling in seconds, and treating both humans and AI agents as first-class participants.

Coin is consumer-first. Non-technical users who have never touched DeFi can create an account, own an earning agent that trades on their behalf, and withdraw profits — without understanding the crypto underneath. No KYC required; pure onchain settlement is the competitive moat. The platform geo-blocks US IPs following the compliance playbook proven by Axiom, Pump.fun, and dYdX.

MVP Phase 1 (4 weeks, near-zero cost): Agent-to-agent P2P trading on Solana mainnet — real escrow-settled trades between AI agents demonstrating the core value proposition. Agents posting offers, accepting offers, settling via escrow, and beating Jupiter prices. This is the fundraising asset to put in front of VCs. All infrastructure runs on free tiers; only cost is ~$200 in mainnet seed capital.

MVP Phase 2 (~3 months, $200–350K post-funding): Full product build with Privy auth, SNS identity, reputation system, consumer UX, 14 listed tokens, and 10–20 Coin-operated market-making agents culminating in public launch.

Team: 5 equal partners (20% each) — satsbased, wes, 2AM, dEXploarer, 0xJ0hnny.eth — aligned and building bootstrapped. Total timeline: ~4 months (1 month MVP Phase 1 + 3 months MVP Phase 2).

2. Problem Statement

DEX Slippage Costs Real Money

AMM-based DEXs price-impact every trade. A $10K swap on a mid-liquidity pool loses 0.5–2% to slippage. Aggregate slippage costs across crypto reached $2.7 billion in 2024. Traders accept this because no alternative exists onchain.

MEV Extraction Steals Value

Sandwich attacks and front-running on Solana (via Jito bundles) systematically extract value from DEX traders. Every swap through Jupiter or Raydium is visible in the mempool and vulnerable to MEV bots that profit at the trader's expense.

No Price Negotiation on AMMs

AMM curves dictate price. Traders take whatever the pool gives. There is no mechanism for two parties to agree on exact terms, making large block trades particularly expensive.

No Reputation or Counterparty Trust

DEX trades are anonymous and atomic — no relationship, no trust accumulation, no way to prefer reliable counterparties. Every trade is a one-off interaction with an impersonal liquidity pool.

Existing P2P Exchanges Don't Serve This Market

Binance P2P, Paxful, and LocalBitcoins are fiat-to-crypto only, require KYC, have slow 15–60 minute settlement windows, and don't support AI agents. They solve a different problem (fiat onramp) for a different user.

AI Agents Have No P2P Venue

AI agents are actively trading tokens on Solana today but are forced through DEXs and Jupiter aggregator — suffering the same slippage and MEV as human traders. There is no P2P marketplace where agents can post offers, negotiate prices, and build trading relationships.

Non-Technical Users Are Excluded

Non-technical users have no way to participate in agent-driven market making without understanding DeFi mechanics, wallet management, and trading strategies. The earning potential of AI agents is locked behind technical barriers.

OTC Desks Serve Institutions Only

OTC desks require trusted intermediaries, high minimums ($100K+), and serve institutions only. Retail traders with $500–$50K trades have no fixed-price execution option.

3. Solution Overview

Coin is a P2P token exchange where sellers post offers and buyers take them. AI agents operate as first-class participants — market makers providing 24/7 liquidity with tighter spreads than AMM pools. Settlement is trustless and atomic via a Solana escrow program using a two-phase commit pattern: the seller locks tokens in an escrow PDA, the buyer sends payment, and the program releases both sides simultaneously.

Identity is anchored on-chain via SNS (.sol domains) and agent identity cNFTs (Metaplex Bubblegum compressed NFTs). A reputation system builds trust over time based on trade history, volume, social verification, and counterparty ratings. Three marketplace types are supported:

  • Agent-to-Agent (A2A): Agents trading autonomously with other agents — the always-on liquidity backbone.
  • Human-to-Agent / Agent-to-Human (H2A/A2H): Humans trading against agent offers, or agents accepting human offers.
  • Human-to-Human (H2H): Direct P2P trades between human users — the classic P2P exchange experience.

All three marketplace types use the same escrow, settlement, and reputation infrastructure. The architecture treats humans and agents as equal participants from day one.

The exchange fee (0.3–0.5% per trade, split between buyer and seller) is deducted at settlement. Built on Solana for sub-second finality at fractions of a cent per transaction. The consumer-first UX hides all crypto complexity from non-technical users.

4. Target Users

4.1 Primary: Non-Technical Users (Consumer)

People who want to earn from AI agents without understanding crypto. They create an account, own an agent that earns for them passively, and can withdraw profits. The crypto infrastructure is invisible. This is the largest addressable market. As the team articulated: "showing people that don't know how to earn from using AI agents by giving them earning agents."

4.2 Primary: Crypto-Native Traders on Solana

Active traders who use Jupiter, Raydium, and Orca daily. They want better execution — less slippage, no MEV, fixed prices for larger trades. They will use Coin when agents offer better prices than DEXs, which they structurally can by quoting tighter spreads than AMM pools.

4.3 Secondary: AI Agent Operators

Developers and power users who want to deploy trading agents. They understand market making and want to earn spread. One agent per user in V1. They bring their own strategies and contribute to platform liquidity.

4.4 Secondary: OTC Traders

People trading large blocks ($10K+) who currently suffer significant price impact on DEXs. P2P gives them fixed-price execution regardless of order size — a $100K trade settles at the same price as a $100 trade.

4.5 Platform Participant: Coin-Operated Agents

At launch, 10–20 platform-operated market-making agents bootstrap liquidity. These quote all supported token pairs at 0.05–0.1% better than Jupiter prices, operate at a capped monthly loss budget as a marketing cost, and will be phased out as third-party agents provide sufficient liquidity.

5. V1 MVP Feature Specification

5.1 Identity & Trust System

5.1.1 Onchain Identity

  • SNS Registration: Every user registers a .sol domain (via Bonfida / Solana Name Service) during onboarding. Users who already have one can link it.
  • Social Verification: Link X (Twitter) and/or Farcaster account for social reputation score. Privy supports both.
  • Agent Creation: Every user gets one agent with a separate .sol domain (e.g., alice-agent.sol) created during onboarding.
  • Agent Identity NFT: Agent registered on-chain with Metaplex cNFT (compressed NFT) bound to .sol domain.

5.1.2 Reputation Score

Every participant (human and agent) has a reputation score:

Formula: R = 0.15T + 0.20V + 0.25S + 0.40C

T = Trade Count: Total completed trades (normalized 0–1 against platform median)

V = Volume: Total USDC-equivalent volume (log-scaled to prevent whale dominance)

S = Social Verification: X account age (40%), follower count (30%), verified badge (30%). For Farcaster: Neynar score. For agents: on-chain identity score.

C = Counterparty Ratings: Average 1–5 star ratings (weighted by recency; last 30 trades count most)

5.3 Trading Mechanics

5.3.1 Listed Tokens (V1 — Day One)

All tokens are traded exclusively against USDC as the sole quote currency. Every listing is a TOKEN/USDC pair.

V1 listed tokens (14): SOL, JUP, PUMP, ZEC, HYPE, SKR, cbBTC, ETH, BONK, PENGU, RAY, EURC, JTO, PYTH

Pair listing policy: Flexible from day one. New tokens are listed when they receive significant user requests or rank among the top traded tokens on Jupiter. Listing new pairs quickly drives exchange volume and revenue. EURC/USDC serves users wanting to buy or sell euro-denominated stablecoins.

5.3.2 Offer Creation

Users specify: token to trade, amount, price per unit (always in USDC), and minimum fill amount (optional, defaults to full amount). Upon creation, the user's tokens are transferred to the escrow program PDA and locked immediately. Offers are visible on the marketplace instantly. Users can cancel anytime before a counterparty accepts.

5.3.3 Offer Acceptance & Settlement

Counterparties (human or agent) browse the marketplace, select an offer, and initiate acceptance. The accepting party's wallet prompts for token approval and transfer to the escrow PDA. A 15-minute timeout begins. If the accepting party doesn't complete within the window, the trade auto-cancels and the locked tokens return. Upon successful payment, the escrow program atomically releases both sides. This flow is identical for A2A, H2A, A2H, and H2H trades.

5.3.4 Escrow Timeout & Anti-Griefing

15-minute timeout protects sellers from liquidity lockup. No penalty for the first timeout, but repeated timeouts (3+ within 24 hours) trigger an automatic flag restricting the buyer for 1 hour.

5.3.5 Price Reference

Each offer displays the current Jupiter aggregator price for the same pair, so buyers immediately see if the P2P offer is better than the DEX. Display shows: "0.08% better than Jupiter" or "0.12% worse than Jupiter".

5.4 Agent Configuration (V1 — Simple)

Agent setup is intentionally simple. After completing their first manual trade, users are prompted: "You just saved $X vs Jupiter. Want an agent to do this 24/7?"

5.5 Marketplace Interface

5.5.1 Two-Sided Market

The marketplace is split into two distinct markets, toggled by a prominent button:

  • Buyers Market: Users who want to buy tokens using USDC. Listings from sellers offering their tokens (SOL, ETH, BONK, etc.) in exchange for USDC.
  • Sellers Market: Users who want to sell tokens to get USDC. Listings from buyers looking to buy tokens by offering USDC.

Liquidity is always and only provided by users buying and selling. There are no automated market makers or liquidity pools — every offer is from a real counterparty (human or agent).

5.5.2 User Trade Preferences

Each user (human or agent) configures their trading preferences through their account:

  • Pair selection: Users choose which token pairs they want to trade. E.g., a user might only trade SOL/USDC and ETH/USDC. Their listings only appear in those markets.
  • Amount limits: Users set minimum and maximum amounts per pair for each token they have available for trades. E.g., "100 SOL available" or "10,000 USDC available" or "2M BONK available."
  • Side selection: A user can be a buyer only, a seller only, or both. E.g., a user with USDC might only want to buy SOL, PUMP, and BONK. A user with SOL and ETH might only want to sell those tokens for USDC.

These preferences apply equally to humans and agents.

5.5.3 Layout & Features

  • Hybrid layout: order-book-style table on desktop, card-based feed on mobile
  • Prominent [Buy | Sell] toggle at the top of the marketplace to switch between buyers and sellers market
  • Both views display: token pair, price per unit (USDC), total amount, counterparty's reputation score and tier badge, type (human/agent), time since posted, and price delta vs Jupiter reference
  • Filtering: token pair selector, price range, minimum reputation, counterparty type (human/agent/all)
  • Sort by: best price, highest reputation, newest, largest amount
  • Default: best-priced offers first

5.6 Live Trade Feed

A real-time feed of completed trades visible to all users. Shows: token pair, amount, price, buyer/seller .sol domains (anonymized if privacy enabled), participant type badges (human/agent), and timestamp. Acts as social proof that the platform is active. Powered by Convex real-time subscriptions.

5.7 Tutorial Agent / Demo Mode

New users can try the platform with a demo mode using fake USDC before committing real funds. A tutorial agent walks them through: browsing offers, accepting a trade, seeing settlement, and setting up their own agent. This is critical for non-technical user onboarding.

5.8 Privacy (Application Layer)

  • Trade metadata (memo, context, notes between parties) encrypted end-to-end
  • Profile visibility controls: trade as agent profile only, hiding human identity
  • Anonymous offer listing: .sol domain hidden until trade is initiated

5.9 Platform

V1 Launch Platform: Mobile-responsive web app (React/Next.js). NOT native iOS/Android apps for V1.

Rationale: Web ships faster, doesn't require App Store approval (crypto apps face review friction), and allows rapid iteration. The web app must feel native on mobile browsers.

6. Coin-Operated Market Making Agents (Cold Start)

6.1 Bootstrap Strategy

Coin deploys 10–20 platform-operated market-making agents at launch to solve the cold-start problem. These agents create visible transaction volume, demonstrate the flywheel in action, and ensure every buyer finds liquidity from day one.

6.2 The Flywheel

More agents listing offers → Better prices for humans → More human volume → More fee revenue for agents → More agents join → Deeper liquidity → Even better prices → Repeat.

Driven by economic self-interest of agent market makers.

6.3 Third-Party Agent Economics

Third-party agents earn spread between buy and sell offers. Example: buy SOL at $134.80, sell at $134.93 — $0.13 spread per SOL is profit, minus the 0.15% per-side exchange fee. For profitability, agents need spreads wider than 0.30% while still beating Jupiter prices.

Additionally, the platform charges a flat 0.05% fee on agent-facilitated trades. This is clearly disclosed in agent documentation.

Coin follows the proven compliance playbook established by Pump.fun, Axiom, Bankr, and dYdX — platforms that operate at scale in crypto without full US regulatory compliance by combining geo-blocking, disclaimers, and pure onchain settlement.

Architecture is designed to support KYC integration if required in the future.

11. Smart Contract Design (Solana Programs)

11.1 Escrow Program (Rust/Anchor)

The escrow program is the core of Coin's settlement layer. It implements a two-phase commit pattern: sellers lock tokens by calling create_offer(), and buyers complete trades by calling accept_offer(). The program holds tokens in PDAs and releases both sides atomically.

InstructionCallerDescription
create_offer(token, amount, price, min_fill)SellerTransfers seller's tokens to escrow PDA; emits OfferCreated event
accept_offer(offer_id)BuyerBuyer sends USDC to escrow PDA; program verifies and releases both sides atomically
cancel_offer(offer_id)SellerReturns locked tokens to seller; only if not yet accepted
claim_timeout(offer_id)AnyoneAfter 15-min timeout, returns tokens to seller; resets offer status
set_fee_rate(rate)Owner (multisig)Updates fee percentage; capped at 1% maximum
withdraw_fees(token)Owner (multisig)Withdraws accumulated protocol fees to treasury

11.2 Security Approach

V1 contract is intentionally simple — minimize attack surface. Security measures:

  • Program is small (~300 instructions equivalent) and auditable
  • Uses well-tested Anchor patterns and SPL token library
  • Reentrancy protection via Anchor's built-in checks
  • Pausability for emergency stops
  • Fee rate cap (max 1%) enforced in program
  • Timelock on admin functions
  • Peer review by team before deployment
  • Per-trade escrow caps at launch (progressive trust limits)
  • Formal audit planned for V2 after revenue justifies cost ($30–80K)

11.3 Agent Identity Program

Implements on-chain agent identity using Metaplex Bubblegum compressed NFTs. Each agent is represented by a non-transferable cNFT bound to the operator's wallet. The cNFT stores agent metadata (name, capabilities, operator). Uses DAS API for reads.

12. Development Timeline

The project follows a two-stage approach: a 4-week bootstrapped MVP to demonstrate core functionality and secure funding, followed by a funded full build to production launch.

MVP Phase 1: Investor Demo (Weeks 1–4) — Near-Zero Cost

The single goal of the MVP is to demonstrate agent-to-agent P2P trading working on Solana — real escrow-settled trades between AI agents, not a mockup.

This is the fundraising asset. Investors need to see the core mechanism functioning: agents posting offers, agents accepting offers, atomic escrow settlement, Jupiter price reference, and the flywheel in action.

Week 1: Escrow Program + Agent Wallets

  • Deploy escrow Anchor program on Solana devnet (two-phase commit: create_offer, accept_offer, cancel_offer)
  • Create programmatic agent wallets (Solana keypairs)
  • Fetch Jupiter aggregator prices for SOL/USDC reference pricing
  • Fund agent wallets with devnet SOL + USDC
  • Milestone: Two wallets can escrow-settle a token swap on devnet

Week 2: Agent Runtime + Offer Engine

  • Build agent runtime (TypeScript): monitor Jupiter prices, calculate spread, post offers
  • Implement offer creation and acceptance via escrow program
  • Deploy 2–3 Coin-operated agents quoting SOL/USDC on devnet
  • Agents trading with each other automatically — posting, accepting, settling
  • Simple backend API (Next.js API routes + Convex) to store/query offers and trades
  • Milestone: Agents autonomously trading with each other on devnet, visible in database

Week 3: Web UI + Live Demo

  • Minimal web app (Next.js on Vercel): marketplace feed showing live agent offers
  • Jupiter price reference displayed alongside each offer
  • Real-time trade feed showing completed agent-to-agent trades
  • Basic wallet connection so a human can accept an agent's offer
  • Trade detail view showing escrow settlement on Solana explorer
  • Milestone: Web app showing live agent trading activity, human can execute a trade

Week 4: Polish + Investor Deck Integration

  • UI polish: loading states, trade confirmation animations, mobile responsive
  • Deploy agents on Solana mainnet with small real funds ($50–200 USDC per agent)
  • Record demo video of agents trading in real-time
  • Prepare investor demo flow
  • Landing page at coin.family with pitch + demo link
  • Milestone: Working product demo on mainnet ready for investor meetings

15. Open Questions

  1. Fee structure: 0.3% total (2AM's model: 0.15% each side + 0.05% agent platform fee) vs 0.5% (original: 0.25% each side). Lower fees attract volume but reduce revenue per trade. Team to model and decide.
  2. Escrow design: Two-phase commit (seller locks first, buyer sends second) vs fully atomic (both fund simultaneously). Two-phase is simpler but has the 15-min timeout problem. Team leans two-phase.
  3. Agent pricing strategy: Static offset from Jupiter vs dynamic based on volatility/volume? V1 uses static; dynamic is V2.
  4. Reputation weight initialization: Current weights (0.15T + 0.20V + 0.25S + 0.40C) need simulation before launch.
  5. Token listing criteria: What volume/liquidity thresholds must a Solana token meet to be listed? Who decides?
  6. Legal entity timing: Wyoming DAO LLC filing before or after funding applications?
  7. Contributor agreement formalization: 20% equal split established — vesting, active contribution requirements, and exit terms must be documented before MVP Phase 2.
  8. Wallet provider: Privy embedded vs Phantom embedded vs Turnkey vs Crossmint. Phase 2a spike determines.
  9. Agent framework: Custom TypeScript agent vs Solana Agent Kit vs ElizaOS-based? Evaluate MVP Phase 1–2a.
  10. x402 threshold: When to use x402 vs escrow for small trades? Current lean: escrow for all user-facing trades; x402 for agent-to-agent service fees only. Revisit V2.
  11. Partial fills: V1 is full-fill only. When to add partial fills? Adds contract complexity. Likely V2.
  12. Jupiter price fallback: If Jupiter reference price is stale or unavailable? Fallback to Pyth/Switchboard on-chain oracle + CoinGecko API. If all fail, agents pause; UI shows warning.
